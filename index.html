§ 02 — The Problem

IP is the most valuable asset that can't be owned.

Think about this for a second: A $100M film franchise sits on one studio's balance sheet. It will never be traded. Its actual value is unknown. The rights are locked in 10-year contracts with gatekeepers who take 50%.

A writer in Lagos tells a story that moves millions online. No one measures it. There's no market for it. When a studio finally notices, it's a fire sale.

A creator builds something extraordinary. But there's no way to access capital, no pricing mechanism, no infrastructure to let ownership flow. They're not founders—they're contract workers.

Meanwhile, equities trade on transparent rails. Real estate has a market. Art has Masterworks. But IP? IP remains fragmented, opaque, and trapped in bilateral deals struck in conference rooms by agents no one can reach.

This is an infrastructure problem, not a platform problem. The world's best stories don't need another discovery tool. They need an operating system—one that makes IP legible, comparable, and eventually tradeable. One that turns IP into an asset class. At KASKAL, we're not building for creators. We're building the rails that creators, studios, and capital all depend on.
